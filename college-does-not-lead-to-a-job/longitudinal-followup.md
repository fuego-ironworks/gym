# Longitudinal alumni follow-up

The published placement number is the beginning of the audit, not the end.

For Berkeley Haas MFE Class of 2025, the program reports:

- 70 students looking for jobs;
- 67 received offers;
- 67 accepted offers.

That answers one narrow question about an early post-graduation window.

It does **not** by itself answer:

- total graduating cohort size;
- why anyone was excluded from "looking for jobs";
- prior work experience and selectivity;
- whether the accepted job was the student's desired occupation;
- whether the student remained in that occupation;
- job duration;
- layoffs, firings, voluntary exits, or visa-related exits;
- total compensation rather than initial base salary;
- hours and working conditions;
- whether the graduate says the degree was worth its cost;
- counterfactual outcome without the degree;
- whether the degree caused the placement or mainly selected people already likely to place well.

## Public-profile follow-up

Use public professional profiles, personal sites, blogs, interviews, alumni pages, and archived material.

For each graduate, record only observable facts:

- pre-program employment;
- internship during the program;
- first documented post-program job;
- current documented job;
- dates where public;
- occupational field;
- whether the person remains in the target field;
- explicit statements about difficulty, satisfaction, regret, value, burnout, job search, layoffs, or leaving the field;
- source and check date.

Do **not** infer mental state, depression, dishonesty, satisfaction, or hidden motives from tone, posting frequency, photographs, likes, résumé language, or apparent self-presentation.

"No negative statement found" does not mean "happy."
"No current role found" does not mean "unemployed."

## Follow-up intervals

Recheck at roughly:

- graduation;
- 6 months;
- 1 year;
- 2 years;
- 5 years;
- 10 years.

The longer-term questions matter more than launch placement:

> Did the initial job turn into a durable career that justified the degree's cost and opportunity cost?

## Causal caution

A selective professional master's can show excellent placement because:

1. the program teaches valuable skills;
2. the program provides employer access;
3. the credential itself has signaling value;
4. the admissions process selects candidates who would have done well anyway;
5. students enter with prior relevant experience;
6. all of the above.

The follow-up data should not assume which mechanism dominates.

## Explicit first-person material

When a graduate publishes something like:

- "the search was harder than I expected";
- "I regret doing the degree";
- "I left finance";
- "the job was not for me";
- "this opened doors I could not have opened otherwise";

cross-link the item to the `regret` branch.

Only use the person's own statement for the sentiment classification.
